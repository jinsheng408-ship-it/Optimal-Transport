Statistical EDA Pipeline

A good analysis never jumps straight to the model. This page walks through the five-step statistical workflow: generate data from a known Gaussian, explore its shape, test distributional assumptions, compare the two samples, and finally compute the optimal transport plan. Each step informs the next.

Step 1 — Generate data & plot

Draw n samples from each Gaussian and overlay the empirical histogram with the true density curve. This is the first sanity check: do the samples look like what the parameters predict?

Step 2 — Explore the trend

Look at the box-and-whisker summary and the rank-sorted scatter plot. These reveal the location, spread, and any unusual data points before formal testing.

Step 3 — Test normality assumption

The Q-Q plot compares empirical quantiles against the theoretical Gaussian. Points on the diagonal confirm normality. The Shapiro-Wilk W statistic quantifies this: W ≈ 1 supports Gaussian, W ≪ 1 suggests departure.

Q-Q sample B — reference line

Step 4 — Compare the two distributions

The empirical CDFs and KS statistic show how different the two samples are. The maximum vertical gap between CDFs is the KS statistic D — a model-free measure of distributional difference.

CDF of sample A CDF of sample B

Step 5 — Optimal transport plan

Now that we have confirmed the data looks sensible, we apply the Sinkhorn algorithm. The heatmap shows \(P\) — how mass in distribution A is matched to B. The computed cost should approach the analytic W₂² as \(\varepsilon \to 0\).
